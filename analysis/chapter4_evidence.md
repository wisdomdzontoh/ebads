## 0. What changed since the first run, and why

**Fix 1 — case origins repositioned.** 16 of 22 distinct case origins sat within 0.0–1.1 km of
a facility — in 15 of the 16 cases, exactly on top of one (0.000 km). Every such origin was
displaced 1.5–4.0 km from the nearest facility (actual distances achieved: 1.50–1.96 km),
along a bearing chosen to keep the point on plausibly populated ground and preserve the
case's stated character (a dense-urban origin stays dense-urban, a peripheral one stays
peripheral) — full rationale per origin in `analysis/raw/case_displacement_plan.json`.
Urgency, bed type, case ordering, and the 9 escalating cases' origins were **not** touched.
Verified computationally, not assumed: the smallest nearest-facility distance across all 22
origins is now 1.500 km (`analysis/raw/geographic_reachability.json` origin list), and the
smallest travel time to any placement across all three strategies in the re-run is 3.0 minutes
(§2.2) — comfortably clear of the 1.0-minute floor a new integration test now asserts
(`test_no_placement_is_within_one_minute_of_travel`, `backend/tests/integration/test_scenario_runner.py`).

**Fix 2 — `mean_facility_attempts` renamed and its denominator corrected.** The measure was
dividing total reservation attempts by *every* case, escalated cases included — despite an
escalated case making no reservation attempt at all (0 attempts, by `AllocationOutcome`'s own
convention). That made the result mathematically identical to `placed / total` (i.e.
`placement_success`), which is why it read 0.700 for every strategy in rev1 and could never
have read the way an attempt count actually behaves (never below 1). Fixed in
`app/scenario/measures.py`: the denominator is now placed cases only, and the field is
renamed `mean_reservation_attempts_per_placed_case` so the denominator is unambiguous in the
output. Verified in this run: it reads exactly **1.000** for every strategy's sequential
primary run (§2.2) — correct, since every placement makes at least one attempt and the
primary run never contends for the same bed twice — and the real, higher values appear only
in the contention burst (§7), where fall-through actually occurs.

Both fixes are additive to the runner/report code (new field, new displaced coordinates); all
24 scenario tests (22 original + 2 new) pass, and two consecutive runs of the fixed case set
remain byte-identical (§1, §5).

## Summary of Findings

- **Fix 1 changes the picture materially for greedy and confirms it for weighted/urgency-
  adaptive.** With real travel distances, greedy's overall mean capability match drops to
  0.800 — now *below* weighted's 0.829, not tied with it as rev1 reported. Weighted and
  urgency-adaptive's headline numbers move only modestly (their scoring already weighed
  travel time, so a change from 0-minute to 3+-minute candidates rarely flips their winner).
- **The critical-at-tertiary geographic ceiling is unchanged and independently re-confirmed
  by the coordinate fix**: still exactly 3 of 9 critical origins (C001, C002, C009) have any
  tertiary facility within 30 minutes; still exactly 6 of 9 (C003–C008) have none. Weighted
  and urgency-adaptive still place exactly 3/9 (33.3%) of critical cases at tertiary, hitting
  that ceiling precisely. **Greedy's rate fell further below the ceiling than rev1 showed**:
  1/9 (11.1%), not 2/9 — real distance revealed that greedy's nearest facility for C009 is a
  secondary hospital 3.9 minutes away, not the tertiary building it happened to be standing
  inside.
- **Escalations are unchanged**: the same 9 cases (C003–C008, C010, C011, C030) escalate under
  all three strategies, with the same fallback facilities and distances — none of their
  origins were touched by Fix 1, and this is verified computationally, not merely asserted by
  construction.
- **E1 is confirmed, more sharply than rev1.** Critical-at-tertiary: 33.3% (weighted/urgency-
  adaptive) vs 11.1% (greedy) — a 22.2-point gap, not 11.1. Additional travel cost on critical
  cases is small: urgency-adaptive 12.1 min vs greedy 10.9 min (+1.2 min).
- **E2 is contradicted, more sharply than rev1.** Standard-tier mean capability match:
  urgency-adaptive 0.500 vs weighted 0.711 (unchanged from rev1 to three decimals). Standard-
  tier mean travel time: urgency-adaptive 38.3 min vs weighted 12.9 min — nearly 3x, and now
  includes the single longest placement in the whole study (82.4 min, C024).
- **The facility-size mechanism is now stated numerically, not argued.** Every one of
  urgency-adaptive's 9 tertiary standard-tier placements sits at a facility with b̂ ≥ 0.8
  (available beds 8–51); every one of greedy's matched primary/secondary picks sits at b̂ ≤
  0.29 (available beds 2–12). The raw bed-count gap is the mechanism (§8, §9).
- **A post-hoc counterfactual (R6, explicitly not an independent confirmation) narrows but
  does not close the E2 gap.** Changing only the standard-tier weight vector to (0.40, 0.35,
  0.25) — travel raised, bed weight cut from 0.50 to 0.35 — moves standard-tier mean travel
  time from 38.3 to 17.9 min and mean capability match from 0.500 to 0.600, roughly half the
  distance back toward weighted's 12.9 min / 0.711. This is evidence the effect is
  attributable in large part to that one vector's bed weight, not to urgency-conditioning as
  a concept, but it is a diagnostic run selected after seeing the E2 result, not a validation
  finding (§10).
- **E3 remains partially supported, not confirmed** — and for a different, sharper reason
  than rev1. Greedy has the shortest mean travel time (5.9 min vs 10.8 and 21.0 min) but its
  capability match (0.800) is neither tied with nor below the others: it now sits *between*
  weighted (0.829, the highest) and urgency-adaptive (0.748, the lowest). Greedy does not
  have the lowest capability match under either run.
- **Contention mode produced zero double bookings under all three strategies again.**
  Attempts per placed case rose for weighted (1.62 → 1.86) and urgency-adaptive (2.43 → 2.52)
  relative to rev1 — real distances create more genuine ties for the top-ranked candidate
  than co-located, zero-distance ones did.
- **`mean_reservation_attempts_per_placed_case` is exactly 1.000** for every strategy's
  primary run, and only departs from 1.000 in the contention burst, confirming Fix 2 behaves
  as intended.
- **No NFR1 latency test exists in the repository** — unchanged from rev1, reported as an
  absence again.

## 7. Contention (R5)

## 13. Assessment of the three expectations (thesis §3.8.3)

### E1 — urgency-adaptive places a higher proportion of critical cases at tertiary facilities than nearest-facility, at some additional travel cost.

**CONFIRMED — more sharply than rev1.**

- Critical-at-tertiary rate: urgency_adaptive 0.333 (3/9) vs greedy 0.111 (1/9) — a 22.2-point
  gap (rev1: 11.1-point gap), and urgency-adaptive is at the geographic ceiling (§11).
- Additional travel cost on critical cases: urgency_adaptive mean 12.1 min vs greedy 10.9 min
  — +1.2 min, small, as E1 anticipates.
- Critical-case capability match is also markedly better under urgency_adaptive (1.000 vs
  0.733) — a wider gap than rev1's 1.000 vs 0.867.

### E2 — urgency-adaptive performs no worse than fixed-weight on standard-urgency cases.

**CONTRADICTED — unchanged in substance from rev1.**

- Mean capability match, standard tier: urgency_adaptive 0.500 vs weighted 0.711 — identical
  to three decimals across both runs, ruling out the zero-distance bug as the source of this
  finding.
- Mean travel time, standard tier: urgency_adaptive 38.3 min vs weighted 12.9 min —
  urgency_adaptive nearly 3x worse, and now includes both of the study's two placements
  exceeding 45 minutes.
- Placement success and escalation rate are identical on this tier for all three strategies
  (0.900/0.100) — the contradiction is about *which* admissible facility is chosen, not
  whether a bed is found.
- Mechanism now stated numerically (§9): every urgency-adaptive standard-tier pick has b̂ ≥
  0.8; the alternative picks it is compared against never exceed b̂ = 0.29.
- A post-hoc diagnostic (R6, §10) shows roughly half of the gap is attributable to the
  standard-tier vector's bed weight specifically, not to urgency-conditioning as a concept —
  reported as a diagnostic finding, not grounds to revise the primary result.

### E3 — nearest-facility produces the shortest mean travel time and the lowest capability match.

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — for a different reason than rev1.**

- Shortest mean travel time: confirmed. Greedy 7.2 min vs weighted 10.8 min vs
  urgency_adaptive 21.0 min.
- Lowest capability match: **not confirmed, and no longer even close to a tie.** Rev1 reported
  greedy tied with weighted (0.819 each); with real distances greedy's capability match
  (0.800) is *between* the other two — higher than urgency_adaptive's 0.748, lower than
  weighted's 0.829. Greedy is not the lowest under either run; urgency_adaptive is, in both.

## 14. Anomalies

1. **Standard-tier bed-count normalisation lets facility size override capability fit**
   (mechanism behind the E2 contradiction and the E3 partial support; now backed by §9's
   numeric table and §10's counterfactual). Not a runner defect — `weighted_score`'s formula
   and `min_max_normalize`'s behaviour both match their own docstrings and docs/03 §3/§5-6
   exactly. `b̂` is min-max normalised on **absolute** available-bed counts within the
   candidate set; a set spanning a 350-bed teaching hospital and a 22-bed polyclinic pushes
   the teaching hospital's `b̂` toward 1.0 by size alone, and at the standard-urgency weight
   vector (w_b=0.50, its largest single term), that outweighs a capability-match disadvantage
   the matrix explicitly rates as the worst fit for that urgency.
2. **C024's 82.4-minute placement is the single longest trip in the entire study** — a
   *standard*-urgency maternity/specialist case, under urgency-adaptive, marginally further
   than rev1's 81.2-minute C024 placement (the origin moved 1.6 km further from University of
   Ghana Medical Centre in the process, which is what changed the number, not a change in
   which facility was chosen). Within the 90-minute standard radius, so not a hard-filter bug,
   but flagged as clinically questionable, and now joined by a second placement over 45
   minutes (C025, 79.2 min — §4).
3. **Greedy's `score` column is not comparable in scale to weighted/urgency_adaptive's** — raw
   travel time in minutes vs a weighted sum of three [0,1] terms. Unchanged observation from
   rev1, re-flagged so it is not mistaken for a Fix 1 side-effect.
4. **Every escalation in this case set is an ICU case, identical across all three strategies,
   and unaffected by Fix 1** — confirmed, not merely carried over, by re-running against the
   repositioned case set (§5). This is a clean result, not an anomaly, but it does mean this
   case set exercises zero strategy-dependent escalation behaviour.
5. **`critical_at_tertiary_rate` still does not discriminate between weighted and
   urgency_adaptive** (tied at 0.333 in both primary and robustness runs) — a hard geographic
   cap both strategies reach exactly (§11), not a runner defect. It does now discriminate more
   sharply against greedy than rev1 showed (0.111 vs the 0.333 ceiling, not 0.222).
6. **Tertiary capacity preservation (§9) still has an n of only 4**, unaffected by Fix 1 (one
   placed at tertiary under every strategy, three escalate) — no discriminating power in this
   case set at this occupancy level, unchanged from rev1.
7. **New to rev2 — C009 flips for greedy specifically.** Before Fix 1, all three strategies
   coincidentally agreed on C009 (because it sat inside the building they all ended up
   picking). After the fix, greedy alone diverges — a clean illustration of exactly the defect
   Fix 1 targets: a colocated origin can silently mask a genuine policy difference by handing
   every strategy the same trivial "nearest facility = itself" answer.
