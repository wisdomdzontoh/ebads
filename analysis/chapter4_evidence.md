## Summary of Findings

- **Escalations are identical across all three strategies.** All 9 escalations (30.0% of the
  case set) are the *same* 9 cases under greedy, weighted, and urgency-adaptive alike — every
  one is a peripheral-origin critical or urgent ICU request with zero admissible ICU
  candidates. This is a geography/capacity ceiling, not a scoring-policy effect (§2.4).
- **The critical-at-tertiary ceiling is geographic, not a scoring artefact.** Only 3 of the 9
  critical-case origins have *any* tertiary facility reachable within the 30-minute critical
  radius at all — regardless of bed availability. Weighted and urgency-adaptive both place
  exactly 3/9 (33.3%) critical cases at tertiary, hitting that ceiling precisely; greedy
  reaches only 2/9 (22.2%), falling short of the ceiling because it ignores tier entirely
  (§2.8, §2.9).
- **E1 is confirmed**: urgency-adaptive places a higher share of critical cases at tertiary
  facilities than greedy (33.3% vs 22.2%) at a small additional travel cost (+0.6 min mean,
  11.8 vs 11.2), and with a better mean critical-case capability match (1.000 vs 0.867) (§3).
- **E2 is contradicted, not partially supported.** On standard-urgency cases, urgency-adaptive's
  mean capability match (0.500) is markedly *worse* than fixed-weight's (0.711), and its mean
  travel time (37.7 min) more than triples fixed-weight's (11.4 min). The mechanism is
  identified in §4: bed-count normalisation is computed on *absolute* bed counts, so within a
  candidate set spanning small polyclinics and large teaching hospitals, the standard-tier
  weight vector's heavy bed-weight (0.50) lets a hospital's sheer size outscore its poor
  capability match for a standard-urgency patient — sending several standard cases to distant
  tertiary facilities the capability matrix itself rates as the *worst* fit for that urgency.
- **E3 is partially supported, not confirmed.** Greedy does produce the shortest mean travel
  time (4.9 min, vs 9.7 and 19.7 min), but it does *not* produce the lowest capability match:
  greedy ties with weighted at 0.819, while urgency-adaptive is lower still at 0.748 — the
  same standard-tier effect above pulls urgency-adaptive's *overall* capability match below
  greedy's.
- **18 of 30 cases (60%) are decided differently by at least one strategy** — not a marginal
  effect. Every one of those divergences traces cleanly to the weight vector in play; none
  looks arbitrary once the t̂/b̂/ĉ decomposition is inspected (§2.5).
- **Contention mode produced zero double bookings under all three strategies** (§2.6),
  confirming the FR8/FR9 compare-and-set fall-through holds under a shared-snapshot burst
  regardless of which strategy is scoring it. Urgency-adaptive's burst needed markedly more
  fall-through (mean 2.43 attempts per placed case, max 6) than greedy's (1.10, max 2) —
  expected, since its scores discriminate less sharply between top candidates for several
  cases, more often putting several cases' first choice on the same facility.
- **The robustness re-run (R4) moves every measure toward the fixed-weight baseline, as
  designed, but does not close the standard-tier capability-match gap** — only narrows it
  (0.500 → 0.567, against fixed-weight's 0.711). No measure reverses direction between the
  primary and robustness weight sets (§2.7).
- **No NFR1 latency test exists in the repository** (§2.10) — reported as an absence, not
  omitted.
- **One reproducibility caveat, reported here rather than left implicit**: the working tree
  that produced this run includes commits beyond `code_commit` in `manifest.json`, plus this
  session's own small addition to `app/scenario/runner.py`/`report.py` (surfacing the two
  escalation fallbacks into `decisions.jsonl`, which did not exist before). See §5.

**Travel model** (`app/domain/travel/live.py` + `haversine.py`, confirmed by reading the
code, not assumed): every run used `LiveTravelTimeService(api_key="")`, so every travel time
is the Haversine great-circle distance divided by a fixed 30 km/h urban speed, converted to
minutes. **No circuity factor is applied** (straight-line distance is used directly, not
scaled up for road indirection) and **no fixed overhead is added** — `COORDINATION_OVERHEAD_MIN`
(5 min, `parameters.py` §8.1) exists but is read only by `app/simulation/engine.py` and
`app/analysis/report.py`, the exploratory discrete-event modules docs/07's banner explicitly
excludes from the reported evaluation; grep confirms neither `app/scenario/` nor
`domain/allocation/service.py` reference it. Every travel time in this document is therefore
a pure straight-line estimate, not a road-network one.

Representative example — **C001** (critical, general bed, dense Osu origin, 15 admissible
candidates):

| Strategy | Facility | Tier | Travel (min) | t̂ | b̂ | ĉ | Score |

weighted's slightly farther one (37 Military, 6.5 min), even though 37 Military's raw score

overrule it. The full 18-case set (with the same t̂/b̂/ĉ breakdown) is in
`analysis/raw/cross_strategy_diff.json`.

## 7. Contention (R5)

All three strategies scored the full 30-case burst against one shared starting snapshot, then
reserved sequentially in file order — exercising the FR9 fall-through deliberately.

| Strategy | Burst size | Cases placed | Cases escalated | Total reservation attempts | Mean attempts / case (all 30) | Mean attempts / placed case | Max attempts (one case) | Double bookings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy | 30 | 21 | 9 | 23 | 0.767 | 1.095 | 2 | **0** |
| weighted | 30 | 21 | 9 | 34 | 1.133 | 1.619 | 3 | **0** |
| urgency_adaptive | 30 | 21 | 9 | 51 | 1.700 | 2.429 | 6 | **0** |

Zero double bookings under all three strategies — the successful-reservation count at every
(facility, bed_type) key never exceeded that key's starting availability, verified for the
full burst, not sampled. Not a defect to report; this is the expected, designed outcome and is
reported as a positive result, not assumed.

Same 9 cases escalate in contention mode as in the primary depleting run (C003-C008, C010,
C011, C030) — consistent with §5's finding that these 9 have no admissible ICU candidate
regardless of bed-state history.

Urgency-adaptive needed substantially more fall-through than greedy (mean 2.43 vs 1.10
attempts per placed case, max 6 vs 2). This is not evidence of a runner defect: its scores
compress differences between top-ranked candidates for several standard-tier cases more than
greedy's raw-travel-time score does (see §4's standard-tier finding and §8's mechanism), so
more cases in the burst rank the *same* facility first, and more of them therefore fall
through to a second, third, or later choice once that facility's beds run out within the
burst. Reconstructed from `analysis/raw/r5_contention_greedy.json`,
`r5_contention_weighted.json`, and the CLI's own `contention.json`.

## 3. Assessment of the three expectations (thesis §3.8.3)

### E1 — urgency-adaptive places a higher proportion of critical cases at tertiary facilities than nearest-facility, at some additional travel cost.

**CONFIRMED.**

- Critical-at-tertiary rate: urgency_adaptive 0.333 (3/9) vs greedy 0.222 (2/9) — higher, and
  at the geographic ceiling (§9).
- Additional travel cost on critical cases: urgency_adaptive mean 11.8 min vs greedy 11.2 min
  — +0.6 min, a small but real increase, exactly "some additional travel cost."
- Not asked for by E1 but consistent with it: critical-case capability match is also better
  under urgency_adaptive (1.000 vs 0.867).

### E2 — urgency-adaptive performs no worse than fixed-weight on standard-urgency cases.

**CONTRADICTED.**

- Mean capability match, standard tier: urgency_adaptive 0.500 vs weighted 0.711 — a 0.211
  absolute gap, urgency_adaptive markedly worse.
- Mean travel time, standard tier: urgency_adaptive 37.7 min vs weighted 11.4 min —
  urgency_adaptive 3.3x worse.
- Both gaps persist (narrowed, not closed) under the robustness weight set (§8): capability
  match 0.567 vs weighted's unchanged 0.711.
- Placement success and escalation rate are identical on this tier (0.900/0.100 for all three
  strategies) — the contradiction is specifically about *which* admissible facility gets
  chosen, not about whether a bed is found at all.
- Mechanism identified in §4/§6/§8: the standard-tier weight vector's heavy bed-count term
  (w_b=0.50) is computed on absolute available-bed counts, which structurally favour large
  tertiary hospitals over small polyclinics regardless of the capability matrix's own explicit
  ranking (which rates tertiary the *worst* fit — 0.5 — for standard urgency, against 1.0 for
  primary). See the anomaly write-up below.

### E3 — nearest-facility produces the shortest mean travel time and the lowest capability match.

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.**

- Shortest mean travel time: confirmed. Greedy 4.9 min vs weighted 9.7 min vs
  urgency_adaptive 19.7 min.
- Lowest capability match: **not confirmed**. Greedy's overall mean capability match (0.819)
  ties with weighted's (0.819, to the precision reported) and is *higher* than
  urgency_adaptive's (0.748). Greedy is not the lowest on this measure; urgency_adaptive is —
  driven by the same standard-tier effect that produces the E2 contradiction (urgency_adaptive's
  standard-tier capability match, 0.500, is the single worst cell of the study and pulls its
  overall average below greedy's).

## 4. Anomalies

1. **Standard-tier bed-count normalisation lets facility size override capability fit
   (mechanism behind the E2 contradiction and the E3 partial support).** Not a runner defect —
   `weighted_score`'s formula (`w_t·t̂ + w_b·(1−b̂) + w_c·(1−ĉ)`) and `min_max_normalize`'s
   behaviour both match their own docstrings and docs/03 §3/§5-6 exactly, verified by reading
   the code, not assumed. The effect is a property of the *policy*: `b̂` is min-max normalised
   on **absolute** available-bed counts within the candidate set. A candidate set spanning a
   350-bed teaching hospital and a 22-bed polyclinic will push the teaching hospital's `b̂`
   toward 1.0 essentially because of its size, not its proportional fullness — and at the
   standard-urgency weight vector (w_b=0.50, the largest single term of any tier), that can
   outweigh a 0.5-point capability-match disadvantage (w_c=0.25) even when the capability
   matrix explicitly rates tertiary the *worst* fit for a standard-urgency patient. Concretely
   (§6): C023 sends a standard general-bed patient 40.9 minutes to Korle Bu (tertiary, ĉ=0.5)
   past Amasaman Hospital at 0 minutes (secondary, ĉ=0.8); C024 sends one 81.2 minutes to
   University of Ghana Medical Centre past a 0-minute local option. This is worth flagging
   prominently for Chapter Five: it is the mechanism, not a coincidence, and it directly
   explains why urgency-adaptive underperforms fixed-weight specifically on the tier the
   capability matrix says should route toward *smaller* facilities.
2. **C024's 81.2-minute placement is the single longest trip in the entire study**, for a
   *standard*-urgency maternity/specialist case, under urgency-adaptive — the opposite of what
   "standard" should imply operationally, even though it is within the 90-minute standard
   radius and therefore not a hard-filter bug. Flagged as clinically questionable, not
   technically wrong.
3. **Greedy's `score` column is not comparable in scale to weighted/urgency_adaptive's.**
   Greedy's score is raw, unnormalised travel time in minutes (docs/03 §4 — it ranks by
   nearest, full stop); the other two strategies' scores are weighted sums of three terms each
   in [0,1]. A reader scanning §6's table left-to-right could mistake this for an inconsistency
   in the runner; it is not — verified against `algorithms/greedy.py` and
   `algorithms/base.py::weighted_score`. Flagged here so it is not re-discovered as a false
   "bug" later.
4. **Every escalation in this case set is an ICU case, and every one is identical across all
   three strategies** (§5). This is a clean result, not an anomaly, but it does mean this
   30-case set exercises *zero* strategy-dependent escalation behaviour — a case set with a
   borderline general/maternity_specialist case near the escalation boundary would exercise a
   code path (the `_race_exhausted_outcome`/hard-filter-vs-scoring interaction on escalation)
   that this run never touches for anything but ICU. Not a defect; a coverage note for anyone
   extending the case set later.
5. **`critical_at_tertiary_rate` measures do not discriminate between strategies once the
   geographic ceiling is hit** — weighted and urgency_adaptive are tied at 0.333 in every
   configuration tested (primary and robustness). This is not a runner defect (§9 shows why:
   it is a hard geographic cap, and both strategies reach it) but it does mean this measure,
   on this case set, cannot distinguish weighted from urgency-adaptive at all — only from
   greedy. A future case set with more geographically-reachable critical origins would be
   needed to separate weighted from urgency-adaptive on this specific measure.
6. **Tertiary capacity preservation (§10, non-critical ICU cases) has an n of only 4**, one of
   which (C026) is placed at tertiary under every strategy and three of which escalate
   entirely — the measure has essentially no discriminating power in this case set at this
   occupancy level. Reported as-is; not a reason to adjust the case set retroactively.
